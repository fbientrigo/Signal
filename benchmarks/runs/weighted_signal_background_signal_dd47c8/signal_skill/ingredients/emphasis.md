# Emphasis and context

## Solves

Direct attention to what matters without hiding the surrounding evidence.

This ingredient covers four closely related jobs:

- **context** — keep relevant background visible but quiet;
- **highlight** — make a small focus subset immediately findable;
- **reference** — show a meaningful threshold, baseline, region, or benchmark;
- **annotation** — label the evidence needed to understand the point.

Keeping these together avoids a catalog of tiny styling ingredients.

## Default strategy

1. draw the full context with low visual weight;
2. redraw or overlay the focus with stronger weight;
3. add a reference only when it changes interpretation;
4. annotate only the few items that carry the argument.

Color is one possible emphasis channel, not the only one. Position, linewidth, marker shape, opacity, and direct labels may be more appropriate.

## Guardrails

- do not make context so faint that it disappears;
- do not highlight many things at once;
- do not add arbitrary reference lines;
- do not annotate every point;
- preserve domain-defined colors when they already carry meaning.
